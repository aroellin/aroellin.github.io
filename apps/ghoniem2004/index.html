How this relates to the original experiment

Ghoniem, Fekete and Castagliola (2004), A Comparison of the Readability of Graphs Using Node-Link and Matrix-Based Representations, Sections 3.4.1–3.4.4.

  • The original 36 participants each saw nine graphs in both representations and answered seven questions per graph: 126 questions per participant. Graph sizes were 20, 50 and 100 nodes, with three edge-count levels.
  • The seven tasks were estimating node count, estimating edge count, finding the most connected node, finding a named node, checking a direct link, finding a common neighbour, and finding a path.
  • Participants first practised on two demonstration graphs. Representation order was randomised. Graph order was randomised within a group of three easier graphs and a group of six other graphs. Endpoints were selected in advance for the three questions involving two nodes.
  • Each question had a 45-second limit. Participants could skip. The session included two five-minute breaks and a ten-minute break between representations. Both interfaces supported red selection and green hover highlighting, including both endpoints when hovering an edge.

Scope of this teaching reconstruction

  • This page implements two tasks and a short four-trial session. Each representation appears in two consecutive trials; which representation comes first is random. Practice can show both views. Timed trials show one view and lock the graph and task controls.
  • New graphs and layouts are bundled. The six-node example is the constructed graph on slide 9. Larger graphs use fixed seeds and exact edge counts m = round(d²n²), following the paper’s printed d = √(m/n²), with d = 0.2, 0.4 or 0.6. The displayed proportion m / [n(n−1)/2] uses the conventional definition for undirected graphs.
  • The original graph files and trial records were not located. These new graphs use Fruchterman–Reingold positions rather than the original neato positions. The original highest-degree boost for the most-connected-node question is omitted because that question is not implemented here.
  • Question targets may differ between representations. The path-entry interface, side-by-side inspection, translucent matrix row/column hover bands, local CSV export and feedback after each answer are teaching additions. The paper does not fully specify its response-entry and path-scoring rules; this page accepts any valid simple path, or a correct “no path” response.
  • The page records new responses. It does not recreate the original participants’ accuracy or response times, or establish a reliable ranking from four trials.
